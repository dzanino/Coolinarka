Jagody aren't strawberries: false friends in shops abroad

15 November 2026 · Ján Šikuta

Central European languages look so alike that they are easy to mix up. One word, and you take home blueberries instead of strawberries. In a shop abroad this happens more often than we would like to admit.

Five words that fool you

  • Jagody (Polish): blueberries, not strawberries, even though jahody means strawberries in Slovak and Czech. Strawberries in Polish are truskawki.
  • Czerstwy chleb (Polish): stale, hard bread, even though the similar Slovak and Czech word čerstvý means fresh. Fresh in Polish is świeży.
  • Dýně (Czech): a pumpkin. The Slovak dyňa is a watermelon, which in Czech you look for as meloun.
  • Horká čokoláda (Czech): hot chocolate. In Slovak the same words mean dark, bitter chocolate, which in Czech is hořká.
  • Hús (Hungarian): meat, although hus means goose in Slovak and Czech. Goose in Hungarian is liba.

And one that will please you: when a Polish host says the soup “pięknie pachnie”, it is a compliment. Pachnie means it smells nice, even though the similar Slovak word páchne means it stinks.

Why a dictionary is not enough

When you shop, you are not looking for a word but for a product in an online store. Each store has its own categories and names, often different from the dictionary.
